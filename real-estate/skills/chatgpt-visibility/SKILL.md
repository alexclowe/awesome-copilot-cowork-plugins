Apply this when an agent wants to know what ChatGPT (or another AI assistant) says about them, whether AI recommends them, how to show up in AI answers, or how to claim their business in ChatGPT.

## What you can and can't see

You have no access to the agent's ChatGPT, and AI answers vary by run, account, and location. Work only from answers the agent pastes in. Never claim you queried another AI, and never present one answer as "what ChatGPT says about you" in general. Patterns across several questions and runs are what count.

## Writing the questions

Write questions the way a real buyer or seller would type them, based on **location, property type, and service**:

- "Who are the top listing agents in [town], [state]?"
- "I'm selling a [condo / single-family home] in [neighborhood]. Which agents should I interview?"
- "Which real estate agents in [town] specialize in [new construction / relocation / investment properties / land]?"
- "Who is a good buyer's agent near [ZIP]?"
- "Is [agent name] a good real estate agent in [town]?" (one direct-name question)
- "What do reviews say about [agent name] at [brokerage]?" (one reputation question)

**Fair Housing — CRITICAL.** Never write questions that describe the buyer or seller by a protected class or a proxy for one: no "for families," "for young professionals," "for retirees," "Christian agent," "agent who speaks [language] for [nationality] buyers," or school-quality framings. Agents may legitimately offer services in another language; if the agent asks for that, phrase it as "agents in [town] who work in Spanish," not by nationality. If the agent supplies a question that steers by protected class, rewrite it neutrally and say why in one line.

## Scoring pasted answers

For each numbered question, record:

| # | Question | Named? (Yes / No / Mentioned only) | Position if a list | Who was named instead | Sources/links cited |

Then summarize:

- **Visibility:** named in X of Y questions. Note which question types (listing, buyer, specialty, direct-name) you showed up for and which you didn't.
- **Who keeps getting named:** competitors named in 2+ answers, with counts. Report names exactly as they appeared. Don't add facts about them.
- **Sources ChatGPT leaned on:** group cited sites (Zillow, Realtor.com, Homes.com, Google reviews, brokerage roster, the agent's site, news, etc.) by how often they appeared. These are the fix list's raw material.
- **Wrong or stale facts about the agent:** an old brokerage, wrong service area, wrong phone number, outdated sales claims. Quote the answer text. Mark anything you can't confirm as "[agent to verify]".

If an answer had no sources, say so; don't guess where it came from.

## The fix list

Order fixes by how often the source appeared and how easy the fix is. Typical items:

1. **Claim the ChatGPT Business Hub profile**, if one exists for them. Open chatgpt.com/business-hub and search their name, team, and brokerage office with the city. Local businesses can currently claim only an existing US listing. They must own the business or be authorized to represent it, so **a brokerage office listing needs the managing broker's OK**. A "Contact support" button means the listing is locked, which is common for franchise offices. After the claim, a Persona identity check unlocks edits, insights, and the blue check.
2. **Correct wrong facts at the source** where ChatGPT found them (the cited profile or page), not just in the ChatGPT profile.
3. **Make name, brokerage, address, and phone identical** across their website, Google Business Profile, Zillow, Realtor.com, Homes.com, and the brokerage roster.
4. **State specialties and service areas in plain words** on the website and profiles, in neutral Fair Housing–safe terms.
5. **Get recent, specific reviews** on more than one site.
6. **Re-run the same questions in 30 days** and compare.

## Honesty rules

- Say plainly that claiming a ChatGPT profile **doesn't change ranking**. OpenAI states that claiming "doesn't change its visibility in ChatGPT" and advertising doesn't influence its answers. Present the fixes as improving the information ChatGPT reads, never as a guaranteed ranking.
- Never promise more leads, a position, or a timeline.
- Don't recommend paying for "ChatGPT ranking" services. If the agent asks about one, explain that claiming is free and nobody can guarantee placement.
- Don't invent competitors, sources, review counts, or sales figures. Use bracketed placeholders for anything the agent needs to fill in.

## Output format

Deliverable first: the scorecard table, the four summary sections, and the numbered fix list. Then, below a horizontal rule, a short **Check before acting** list covering only items that apply: facts marked "[agent to verify]," whether they're authorized to claim the listing, and a note that results reflect these runs only.
